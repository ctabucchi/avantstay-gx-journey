In-Stay Communication—First Draft

What actually happens, message by message.

A first-draft map of the real guest communication flow, from booking confirmation through the post-stay review ask—which channel a guest is on, what message they get and when, and who's actually on the other end. Built from real examples, with guest names, phone numbers, and addresses replaced by placeholders since this is a public page.

Applies to vacation rentals and front-desk hotels alike—wherever "the local team" appears below, read it as front desk staff at a front-desk hotel. No-front-desk hotels don't have that on-site equivalent, so that half of the hotel picture is still unmapped.

Direct booking, start to finish

The full guest journey for a direct booking

Applies to: all properties except hotels with front desks

Every message a guest who books directly with AvantStay can receive, in order, from the moment they book to the post-stay survey. It combines the operational touchpoints documented on this page with the concierge marketing track. Conditional messages show their trigger, so not every guest gets every card.

Specialized tracks that run alongside

These are sent by hand by the concierge team for specific guests, instead of or on top of the automated concierge messages above.

  • VIP guests

    Trigger: $15k+ or Voyage booking, flagged by a Salesforce report

    Manual email to set up a discovery call with a concierge agent, then a custom proposal. Focus is on re-booking.

  • Estates guests

    Trigger: booking tagged as Estates in Tapechart

    Manual email and SMS from a dedicated Estates concierge: service list, upgrades, an in-person greeting and full coordination.

  • Corporate offsites

    Trigger: Sales introduces the booking to a dedicated concierge agent

    Discovery call, custom proposal, then upgrades and the stay coordinated with a retention focus.

  • Members

    Trigger: membership sign-up

    Introduction email to a VIP-team concierge agent.

  • Short-term campaigns

    Trigger: a brief in Iterable for target dates or regions

    Supervisor or manager approves the brief, then the campaign goes out manually to each matching guest.

24 hours before check-in

A new thread appears

24 hours before check-in, the guest gets a welcome text sent via SpokePhone from "the local team"—a different number than anything they've seen so far, so it shows up as a brand-new text thread on their phone, separate from whatever OTA or booking-confirmation messages came before.

SMS, via SpokePhoneSent by the local team, 24 hours before check-in
Hi [Guest Name]! Welcome to [Property Name]! We are the local AvantStay team and we're so excited to host you. Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions! We'll be available from [local team hours] at this number. If you have an after-hours emergency please call our awesome customer experience team at (833) 442-8268. We hope you have a wonderful stay!

This is the first point in the journey where the guest is talking to a real local team, not a centralized/automated sender—worth noting given the local-team-identity idea from the brand voice feedback earlier in this map: this touchpoint already does that, it's the rest of the pre-arrival flow that doesn't yet.

Current direction pushes this further: messages should be prescriptive, not passive—something closer to "please walk through the property and let us know if anything comes up" instead of a general "don't hesitate to reach out."

A few hours after check-in

The wellness check

A short "how's it going?" message via SpokePhone, with a simple thumbs-up or thumbs-down reply, is moving to a few hours after check-in rather than the next morning. The open piece: routing a thumbs-down straight to an immediate human response, not just logging it.

In-stay

Who the guest is actually talking to

Where an in-stay message or issue lands depends on the booking channel, and who picks it up depends on whether it escalates.

  1. Guest reaches out during the stay

    Channel depends on how they booked.

    Airbnb → the Airbnb message thread, which only GX (not the field team) has access to.

    Any other OTA → that platform's own guest portal (the VRBO portal, the Booking.com portal, etc.).

  2. Guest reports an issue → GX creates the ISGI

    An ISGI is specifically an issue from a guest who is currently checked in—anything a guest shares after checkout is tracked as feedback, not an ISGI.

  3. Field takes ownership

    Field acknowledges in the case comments and owns all guest communication via Spoke through resolution. "Field" is the internal name for the same team the welcome text calls "the local team."

  4. GX closes the loop

    GX tells the guest field has it. Once handed off, GX monitors to confirm field has actually taken over the conversation, rather than assuming the handoff worked.

  5. If the guest goes quiet on Spoke

    Field asks GX for help communicating status—GX has the OTA-thread access field doesn't, so it can close the loop back through whichever channel the guest actually responds on.

Who owns what, once an ISGI is open

  • GX owns

    • Creating and triaging the ISGI case
    • All compensation conversations with the guest
    • Backup point of contact when Spoke isn't viable (international numbers, Airbnb-only guests)

  • Field owns

    • Guest communication via Spoke, acknowledgment through resolution
    • Keeping the case updated with resolution details
    • Flagging context back to GX in the case when compensation may be needed
Exception: compensation conversations always route back to GX, no matter who's been handling the day-to-day conversation. Field logs what happened in the case comments so GX can decide fairly.

Closing this channel split is a top current priority: one unified thread where the local team can respond to a guest on whatever channel they're actually on—Airbnb, another OTA's portal, or SpokePhone directly—rather than the channel deciding who can even see the message.

Check-out day

The review ask, from more than one direction

Checkout triggers several review-related touchpoints at once, not all of them from AvantStay.

  • From AvantStay (Voyage)

    • A checkout message asking the guest to leave a review
    • A separate message asking for an NPS score
    • Further follow-up SMS/messages asking for a review, if the stay scores as neutral-to-good (see below)

  • From the OTA directly

    • If the guest booked through an OTA, that platform sends its own review request too—outside AvantStay's control, on its own timing

How the "neutral-to-good" call gets made: the stay is scanned for any negative sentiment during the guest's time on property. If none is found, it's scored at 50% confidence as a neutral-to-good stay, and that clears the bar to solicit a review. The full logic for cadence and message variants beyond that single threshold lives in a separate Review Solicitation Journey Mapping board in Figma. Worth pulling the key decision points from that board into this page directly in a future pass, rather than just linking out.
