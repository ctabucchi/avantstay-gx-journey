Hotels—Reviews & Revenue

Ten hotels, two goals that move together.

Baseline segmentation across all ten AvantStay hotels, split by the one thing that actually changes the guest experience here: whether the property has a front desk. Built to support both halves of the goal at once—#1 on TripAdvisor and 5 stars on Expedia/Google, and the revenue performance in the tables below. They're not in tension: a guest who leaves a bad review is usually also the guest who doesn't rebook.

Two operating models

The same brand, two very different check-ins

Four properties run a staffed front desk. Six run remote—a guest lets themselves in, and AvantStay shares revenue with the owner. That split, not guest type, is the primary lens for this map: it's the biggest single driver of what can go wrong before a review gets written.

  • Front-desk

    Sense28 · The Gilmore · The Code · The Mara

    Staffed properties—a person hands over the room, not an app.
    ~24,000 reservations, ~89% of total value · ~$11M · average stay ~2.2 nights
    Wants
    A human, in-person handoff that feels effortless—the thing a front desk is supposed to guarantee.
    Risk
    The Gilmore alone is ~45% of all hotel reservation value—its front desk quality effectively sets the portfolio's review average.
  • No front desk · revenue-share

    Blackhaus · The Wesley · The Monkey Tree · Hotel El Cid · The Rosalie · The Weekend

    Remote check-in, no staffed lobby—closer in operating model to a vacation rental than a hotel.
    ~2,700 reservations, ~11% of total value · ~$1.4M · average stay ~2.2 nights
    Wants
    Clear, self-serve access instructions—there's no desk to fall back on if something's confusing.
    Risk
    Ticket categories here skew toward contact-info and check-in/access questions—exactly the friction a front desk would normally absorb in person.
Who we serve

Six guest segments, built from channel and loyalty status

Built the same way as the vacation rental personas—combining segments that plausibly describe one real kind of guest, not a single verified query. The difference: rental personas leaned on party size and trip value, but those barely vary at hotels (95% of reservations are under $3K, 99.8% are single-unit). Booking channel and operating model are where the actual signal is here, so that's what defines these six.

  • #1 by value

    The Direct Front-Desk Regular

    Books straight from avantstay.com at a staffed property. The largest single segment in the file.
    ~8,400 reservations, ~42% of front-desk value · ~$4.8M · ticket-to-value index ~1.0, proportional
    Wants
    Recognition for skipping the OTA, and a staffed handoff smooth enough to make booking direct feel worth it.
    Risk
    Already the largest, most proportional segment—the risk here is complacency, not crisis. Front-desk quality for this guest effectively is the brand for nearly half of all hotel value.
  • #2 by efficiency

    The Expedia Front-Desk Guest

    Books via Expedia at a staffed property. The quietest segment in the portfolio.
    ~7,700 reservations, ~26% of front-desk value · ~$3.0M · lowest ticket load of any channel, ~0.6 tickets/reservation
    Wants
    A smooth in-person check-in. This guest doesn't ask for much beyond that, and the data backs it up.
    Risk
    Low risk today, but worth understanding why it's the most efficient channel—that's a pattern worth replicating elsewhere, not just leaving alone.
  • #3 by friction

    The Airbnb No-Front-Desk Guest

    Books via Airbnb at a remote-check-in property—the highest-risk combination in the portfolio.
    ~1,100 reservations, ~43% of no-front-desk value · ~$590K · ticket-to-value index ~1.6, the highest flag in the file
    Wants
    Clear, self-serve check-in instructions, and a fast way to reach someone if the lockbox code doesn't work.
    Risk
    Airbnb's top ticket categories are contact-info and check-in/access requests—exactly the friction a staffed desk absorbs in person, concentrated at the properties with no desk to catch it.
  • #4 by consistency

    The Booking.com Guest

    Books via Booking.com—shows up at roughly the same rate at both operating models.
    ~3,200 reservations, ~13% of value at both front-desk and no-front-desk properties · ticket-to-value index ~1.3, high
    Wants
    Straightforward confirmation and check-in details, without needing to contact support to get them.
    Risk
    The over-index holds steady regardless of operating model—this reads as a channel-level pattern (message clarity, timing) rather than a property-specific one.
  • #5 by friction per booking

    The VRBO Guest

    Books via VRBO. The smallest channel by volume, but the highest support load per booking of any source.
    ~490 reservations, ~3% of value · ~$340K · ~1.8 tickets per reservation, the highest of any channel
    Wants
    The same fast reassurance an Airbnb guest wants—VRBO skews similarly self-serve.
    Risk
    Too small to move portfolio averages, but the highest per-booking friction in the file. Worth a dedicated look precisely because volume won't hide the problem.
  • #6 by upside

    The Repeat & Member Hotel Guest

    Has stayed at an AvantStay hotel before, or is enrolled in the membership program.
    ~3,500 repeat reservations, ~14% of value at ~0.75 tickets/reservation, the most efficient guest in the file · members are still just ~1% of value
    Wants
    Recognition of history across stays—the same ask as the repeat vacation-rental guest.
    Risk
    The most efficient guest in the portfolio, and the loyalty program still isn't capturing it: active members are only ~1% of value, barely different from the vacation rental side's ~2%.

Blackhaus's grassroots recovery is already working—apply it to the Rosalie next. Blackhaus moved from 3.8 to 4.1 on TripAdvisor and 3.6 to 3.8 on Google in the last 14 days alone, from a deliberate push to drive reviews. It's still last on TripAdvisor rank (#60/79) and lowest by far on NPS (51.3—next-lowest is 75), so the push needs to continue, but it's proof the playbook works. What this map still doesn't have

The scorecard answers reviews. These are what's left.

The reputation scorecard above covers the review side of the goal with real numbers. What's left is narrower and more specific: closing the one property with no TripAdvisor listing, wiring the tracker into Chef so this stops requiring a manual screenshot, and connecting reviews to revenue and tickets so both halves of the goal reinforce each other.
